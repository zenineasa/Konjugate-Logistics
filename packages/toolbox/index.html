      2 Network

      Pick a role above the map (or press 1 to 6) and click the map to place sites; they snap to the nearest road. Links are suggested. Click a site or a link to change it here or beside it on the map, right-click for its menu, and press ? for the keyboard shortcuts. Everything can be undone. Goods are counted in pallets; a port's containers as ten pallets each.

        Vehicles

        The types every link chooses from. A link to a warehouse runs on heavy trucks, to a store on medium trucks and to a dark store on mini-vans, until you choose others on its card (or press V). Each figure is assumed until you set your own fleet's; costs are in your cost units.

          Categories

          The kinds of goods the network carries. Every supplier, port, store, dark store and customer area carries them in their usual shares until you give it a mix of its own on its card, and a supplier takes each category's lead time until you give it its own. Goods that need refrigerated vehicles go only by the vehicle types ticked Refrigerated. In the model each category is its own copy of the network, with vehicles and room of its own. Each figure is assumed until you set yours.

            Holidays and peaks

            The network's calendar of dated events: a public holiday, a festival, a seasonal peak. Each changes what every store and customer area sells while it lasts, by category, and in the days before it (people stock up) and after it; and the suppliers may not dispatch while it lasts. The days are days of the run (day 0 is a Monday), and every size is yours: nothing published says how much a festival moves your sales. An event is part of the plan, so it is in the baseline as in every scenario: run the network As planned to see what it does, and again with more stock cover to see what holding more saves.

              Money

              What your costs and values are in. The toolbox converts nothing: it writes this beside every sum. The figures a network starts with (a vehicle's costs, the value of a pallet sold) are placeholders in no currency, labelled assumed: replace them with your own, in this money.

              Travel times

              The time each link takes door to door (loading, the drive and unloading), where you know it better than the route: type it here or on the link's card (T), paste a column from a spreadsheet, load a file, or read it off Google Maps or OpenStreetMap and type it back. A time of yours replaces the route's estimate in the model. Say when it holds: a road at the morning peak is not the road at night.

              Suggestions from public data

              Nothing is fetched until you ask. Each source shows what it finds as hollow sites on the map; click one, or Adopt it here, to place it in your network with what was found there.

                3 Model

                4 Scenarios

                Each scenario forks the model on the day it starts and runs beside the baseline.

                Fewer ships through a strait or canal for a while: the ports that depend on it receive less.

                The network as it is planned, with no disruption: its sites' hours and its holidays and peaks. What runs out, what is lost and wasted and what it costs, counted from the day below to the end of the run (the days it lasts are not used). Run it, change a stock cover, a shift or a fleet, run it again, and the two are set side by side.

                A road closed, or restricted, for a while. Vehicles already on it finish their trips.

                Fewer vehicles (drivers short, vehicles off the road) or more (hired). They are hired or released over a few days; only idle ones are released.

                Stores, dark stores and customer areas sell more for a while, as on peak days.

                A supplier makes less than is ordered from it, takes longer over its orders, or both. Nothing ordered is dropped: it waits, arrives late, and comes ready close together once the trouble ends. Lateness reaches what is already in production too.

                A warehouse out of action, or a store closed, for a while: nothing goes into it or out of it by road, and nothing is sold or delivered from it. Goods bound for it wait where they are, and come when it reopens.

                Which failure hurts most? Each one is run in turn for the days below, on its own, and they are ranked by the sales they lose. A comparison of failures of the same length, not a forecast of which will happen.

                Would a new warehouse help? Place it, tick A candidate on its card, and link it to the suppliers and stores it would take. The network as it is and the network with each candidate open are then built and run for the days below, in normal weeks and under the disruption chosen, and set side by side: what each costs a month with its fixed costs, what the disruption costs, and whether the candidate earns its keep. A comparison of designs, in your own figures for rent and the cost to open; not a forecast.
